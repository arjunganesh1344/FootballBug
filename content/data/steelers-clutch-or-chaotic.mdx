Every Steelers fan has a story. A two-possession lead dissolved in the fourth quarter, a late drive by the other team, a field goal or touchdown that makes the final score look way more respectable than it should. Then somehow Pittsburgh wins. Their quarterback scrambles, or T.J. Watt forces a fumble, or something just *happens*.

The narrative is that the Steelers are gritty, battle-tested, clutch. And the record backs it up — kind of. But spend some time in the play-by-play data from 2020 through 2025 and a more complicated picture emerges.

Pittsburgh isn't just winning close games. They're *manufacturing* them.

## Part 1: The Record is Real

Let's establish the baseline. Since 2020, across the regular season, Pittsburgh has been the best team in football in games decided by one score (8 points or fewer).

![One-Score Game Win % (2020–2025 Regular Season)](/images/steelers-clutch/one-score-record.png)

**44 wins and 19 losses** in one-score games. A 69.8% win rate. No other team in the league is close — Kansas City is second at 64.4% and Philadelphia is third at 64.3%. Arizona sits at the bottom at 32.7%, meaning the Steelers are more than twice as likely to win a tight game as the Cardinals.

This is over 63 one-score games across six seasons. That's a large enough sample that you can't wave it away as noise. Pittsburgh has genuinely been elite at finding ways to win games that came down to the wire.

So the clutch narrative has legs. The Steelers show up when it matters.

The question is *why* it keeps mattering in the first place.

## Part 2: The Heart Attack Factory

Here's where the story gets more complicated.

For each game, we used play-by-play data to track the maximum lead any team held during the second half. A **heart attack win** is defined as: the team won, held a 14+ point lead at some point in the second half, and the final margin was 8 points or fewer.

In other words — you were up two scores comfortably, and somehow it ended as a nail-biter.

![Heart Attack Wins by Team (2020–2025 Regular Season)](/images/steelers-clutch/heart-attack-wins.png)

Pittsburgh has **11 heart attack wins** since 2020, tied for second-most in the NFL. Only Philadelphia has more (12, across a much larger win total). That number alone is eyebrow-raising, but the more damning figure is the rate.

The Steelers had 24 games in this span where they held a 14+ point lead in the second half. Eleven of those 24 ended as one-score wins. That's a **45.8% heart attack rate** — tied for the highest in the NFL.

Read that again: nearly half the time Pittsburgh had a comfortable two-score cushion in the second half, they let the game get tight. For comparison:

- **Kansas City** had 35 big-lead games in this span. Only 6 turned into heart attacks — a 17.1% rate.
- **Baltimore** had 44 big-lead games (most in the league). Only 8 became heart attacks — an 18.2% rate.
- **San Francisco** had 45 big-lead games. Eight heart attacks — 17.8%.

Those are teams that know how to step on a throat. Pittsburgh is the team stomping on the gas, then somehow ending up in a footrace.

Some specific Pittsburgh games from this stretch that should give Steelers fans flashbacks:

- **2020 Week 7 at Tennessee**: Led by 20 in the second half. Won 27-24.
- **2021 Week 9 vs. Chicago**: Led by 14. Won 29-27 — a two-point game.
- **2024 Week 13 at Cincinnati**: Led by 17. Won 44-38 in a shootout.
- **2025 Week 4 vs. Minnesota**: Led by 18. Won 24-21.

These are not flukes. They are a pattern.

## Clutch or Chaotic?

The most honest way to frame this is on a scatter plot. One axis is one-score win percentage (how good are you in close games); the other is what share of your total wins were heart attack games (how often do you create those close games unnecessarily).

![Clutch or Chaotic? One-Score Win% vs Heart Attack Rate](/images/steelers-clutch/clutch-vs-chaotic.png)

Pittsburgh lives firmly in the top-right quadrant: **Clutch and Chaotic**. They win tight games at the highest rate in the league, and they manufacture a disproportionate share of those tight games themselves.

Kansas City, for context, is in the top-left quadrant: high one-score win percentage and a *low* heart attack rate. They win close games, but they also protect leads cleanly. That's the difference between a team that's truly clutch and a team that's clutch out of necessity.

The Steelers, to their credit, keep pulling it off. The T.J. Watt strip-sack comes at the right moment. The defense tightens when it needs to. Something works. But the thing is — that something shouldn't need to work if the offense hadn't spent the fourth quarter turning a two-touchdown cushion into a one-possession game.

## The Bottom Line

Since 2020:

- Pittsburgh is **#1 in the NFL** in one-score win percentage at 69.8% (44-19).
- They are **#2 in the NFL** in raw heart attack wins (11).
- Nearly **half** of the games where they held a 14+ point second-half lead ended as a one-score game — the highest rate in football.

The Steelers are genuinely good at winning close games. They are also, with remarkable consistency, the reason those games are close to begin with. Whether that is a testament to their clutch gene or a warning about their inability to close out opponents cleanly probably depends on how you feel about Pittsburgh.

But the data is pretty clear: they aren't just finding clutch moments. They're creating the need for them.

## Methodology

Data sourced from `nfl_data_py`, covering 2020–2025 NFL regular seasons (1,615 games). One-score games defined as final margin ≤ 8 points. Heart attack wins require: (1) team won, (2) team held a 14+ point lead at any point in the second half (quarters 3–4) based on score differential from play-by-play, and (3) final margin ≤ 8 points. Overtime games are included — a team can hold a big lead in regulation and still lose in OT, but those would not count as a heart attack win under this definition. The 2025 season includes regular season weeks 1–18.
