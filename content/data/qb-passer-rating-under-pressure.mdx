## Why Pressure Stats Tell the Real Story

Passer rating is a flawed stat when used in aggregate. A quarterback can post a 105.0 rating and still be a fraud — if he's getting clean pockets behind a dominant offensive line, that number is telling you more about his blockers than him.

The real filter: **passer rating under pressure**.

Take away the clean throws and see what's left.

## The Numbers

The league average passer rating when pressured sits around 72.4. Below average is expected — pressure is bad for everyone. But the separation between quarterbacks becomes stark.

| Quarterback | Clean Pocket | Under Pressure | Delta |
|-------------|-------------|----------------|-------|
| Mahomes     | 118.2       | 94.1           | -24.1 |
| Burrow      | 115.8       | 88.7           | -27.1 |
| Stroud      | 112.4       | 61.3           | -51.1 |
| Goff        | 107.9       | 55.2           | -52.7 |

The delta column is where it gets interesting.

## What the Delta Tells You

Mahomes loses 24 points under pressure. That's still a 94.1 rating — better than most QBs ever achieve with a clean pocket. He's operating at an elite level even when things break down.

Goff loses 52 points. That's not a knock on Goff — the Lions built their offense to protect him and it works brilliantly. But it tells you something important about **scheme dependency**.

## The Takeaway

When evaluating a quarterback, split every stat by pressure vs. clean pocket. The delta is your signal. Elite QBs have small deltas and high floors when pressured. Scheme-dependent QBs have massive deltas and are one bad offensive line injury away from disaster.
